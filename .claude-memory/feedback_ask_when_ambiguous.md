When Raul's request can be read more than one way, or I am not sure what he means, I must ask a short clarifying question **before** making any edit. Guessing, editing, then explaining afterwards is not acceptable. His words: "ha nem vagy biztos..vagy nem érted..kérdezz vissza.. ne lőj aztán kérdezz."

**Why:** Raul writes quickly, often in shorthand and with typos, so his phrasing is frequently ambiguous — but he knows exactly what he wants visually. A wrong guess costs a full round trip (edit → rezip → he reinstalls → he re-screenshots) and looks like I ignored him. Concrete case (2026-09-16): he asked to shrink "az egész box" of the listing gallery to 70%; I shrank only the inner gallery while the outer panel stayed full width, and he replied "szerinted sikerült? mert szerintem nem". One question ("a külső panelt is, a címmel együtt?") would have prevented it.

**How to apply:** Ambiguity about *what* to change → ask first, do nothing. Ambiguity only about a detail of *how* (exact px, shade) → pick a sensible value, do it, and say what I picked so he can correct it. Keep the question to one or two lines with concrete options, not a questionnaire — he wants speed, see [[feedback_no_self_testing]]. This is the companion to [[feedback_scope_stop_rule]]: that one is about not touching unrequested things, this one is about not acting on an unclear request.
